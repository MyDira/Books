# THE HOLD SYSTEM — Core Magic
*Canon. Changes here require a full-series consistency pass. See `canon/power-ledger.md` for who stands where.*

---

## 1. THE WILD

Magic is not rare, secret, or supernatural. It is **weather**.

The **Wild** is the ambient power that saturates everything on the Sill — stone, rain,
bread dough, cattle, grief. It pools in low places and thins on high ones. It runs
in currents like wind. Farmers read it the way farmers read clouds. Bakers proof
dough in a Wild-rich cellar because it rises faster. Midwives can tell a hard birth
by the way the Wild snags in a room.

Nobody in the Sill thinks the Wild is magic, any more than a fish thinks water is
plumbing. It is simply one of the things the world is made of.

**Wild is measured in `weight`** — a unit as ordinary as pounds. A loaf of bread
carries a fraction of a weight. A grown man carries about one. A hunting cat, three.
A Marcher-rank practitioner carries thousands, and the ground knows it.

### Wild-density regions
| Term | Density | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Thin ground** | below 0.4 | high plains, deserts, salt flats. Bad for growing, bad for practice. |
| **Fair ground** | 0.8–1.5 | most farmland, most towns. |
| **Rich ground** | 2–8 | old forests, river deltas, battlefields, ruins. |
| **Welling** | 20+ | a wound in the world where the Deep Wild comes up. Monsters breed here. |

## 2. THE HOLD

Every living thing has an interior. In most people it stays shut.

**Kindling** is the act of opening it — traditionally done at fourteen, with a lit
taper, a witness, and a Ledger clerk to write down what comes out. What opens is a
**Hold**: a real interior country, with ground and sky and weather, which the owner
can walk in when they close their eyes.

A Hold is not a metaphor and not a soul. It is a place. It has an *area*, a *weight*,
and a *terrain*, and all three can be measured by a competent assessor.

> **The typical first Hold** is a room. Four walls of some material that means something
> to the owner, a floor, a ceiling, and one lit fire in the middle of it. Perhaps twelve
> feet square. Perhaps eighteen if the family is well-fed and the kindling was done in
> rich ground.

### The three numbers
Every Hold is graded on three axes. The Ledger prints them on your tally in this order.

1. **Acreage** — how much ground your Hold covers. Determines *how much you can hold*:
   how many terrains you can support, how many techniques you can carry, how long you
   can fight before you are running on empty.
2. **Weight** — how much Wild your Hold contains per acre. Determines *how hard you hit*.
   A heavy Hold is dense, saturated, dangerous. This is the number people brag about.
3. **Coherence** — how well the terrain fits together, graded *ragged / settled / true*.
   Each grade has sub-classifications (*ragged, first class* through *fourth*) which
   exist because the Ledger cannot bear an unsubdivided category, and which are the only
   way most practitioners ever measure progress.
   A ragged Hold leaks, wanders, and fails under pressure. A true Hold does what it is
   told. This is the number that actually wins fights, and almost nobody talks about it.

**Total output ≈ settled acreage × weight × coherence.** Practitioners argue about the
formula in taverns the way other worlds argue about sports.

### The fourth number: SETTLED ACREAGE
Weight is **not uniform across a Hold.** The brass frame reads the *peak* — the densest
coherent region — because that is what determines output at the point of use. Ground
that carries no weight is *unsettled*: it is yours, it counts toward acreage, and it does
nothing whatever.

For ordinary practitioners this distinction is meaningless. A nine-foot room settles
entirely within a season and nobody thinks about it again, which is why the Ledger's
form has three boxes and not four.

For anyone whose acreage badly outruns their weight, it is the whole of their life.
Raising weight across ground is slow, expensive, and unglamorous, and it is the only
thing that turns acreage from a curiosity into power.

> **Wick's arithmetic, the whole series in one line:** he has more ground than a
> Dominion and less weight than a loaf of bread. Every book is a fight to settle
> another piece of it.

### WEIGHT BANDS (locked — every number in 25 books measures against these)
Weight is Wild density, in the same unit used for ordinary matter. A grown adult body
carries about **1.0**. A loaf of bread, about 0.004.

| Rank | Hold weight (peak) | Typical *settled* acreage |
|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | 0.2 – 1.5 | 0.002 (a nine-foot room) |
| **Hearth** | 1.5 – 5 | 0.004 – 0.01 |
| **Field** | 5 – 18 | 0.02 – 0.1 |
| **Wold** | 18 – 90 | 0.1 – 0.6 |
| **March** | 90 – 600 | 0.6 – 4 |
| **Realm** | 600 – 5,000 | 4 – 30 |
| **Dominion** | 5,000 – 60,000 | 30 – 200 |
| **Sovereign** | self-generating; the frame reads nothing | — |

Rank is conferred on the **lower** of the two columns, not the higher. This is why a
freak with enormous acreage and no weight is *unrankable* rather than terrifying, and
why the Ledger has never had to think hard about it before.

### WHAT EACH RANK ACTUALLY REQUIRES (the thresholds, stated plainly)
- **Ember** — a Hold that opens, and a fire lit in it.
- **Hearth** — **the Hold's walls hold under pressure.** An assessor applies load; the
  boundary must not deform. *A Hold with no walls cannot be graded Hearth at any weight,
  and this has never come up, because every Hold is a room.*
- **Field** — the Hold gains a **horizon**: a settled boundary far enough out that the
  ground between is continuous. This is what produces Farsense.
- **Wold** — the Verge steps outside the skin. Requires walls that can be *carried*.

### The Verge
Your Hold is inside you, but it *presses outward*. The radius at which it can impose
itself on the real world is the **Verge**.

- Below Wold rank, the Verge stops at the skin. You can only do things to yourself and
  to what you touch.
- At Wold, the Verge steps outside you — a few feet of ground that is *yours*, where
  your terrain is partly true and everyone else's isn't.
- Every rank after that is, functionally, an argument about whose Verge wins where.

Two practitioners meeting is two weather fronts colliding. You can *feel* the stronger
one — the air gets ideas. This is why nobody needs a scouter or a status screen to know
they are outmatched. They simply know, the way you know a storm is coming.

## 3. GROWING A HOLD — the Loop

You cannot grow a Hold by sitting still. Empty ground stays empty. A Hold grows in
exactly three ways, and every practitioner's life is organized around them.

### (a) SEEDS — the main engine
Wildborn — monsters — are creatures whose Holds have grown *outside* them; they are
walking terrain. Kill one, cut it open, and inside is a **Seed**: a compressed knot of
its country, still holding the shape of the thing it belonged to.

**Plant the Seed in your own Hold and that terrain grows there.**

- A frost-wolf's Seed plants a stand of black pine and a wind that never stops.
- A cinder-boar's Seed plants a scorched hollow that smokes for a year.
- A river-drake's Seed plants a river, and the river will find its own bed.

What grows is never quite what you were promised. The Seed brings the *kind* of terrain;
your existing ground decides the *particulars*. Plant a river in a salt flat and you get
a salt river. Plant the same Seed in a pine wood and you get a cold clear creek and better
trees. **This is why no two practitioners are ever the same, and why copying someone
else's build does not work.**

Seeds are graded by the rank of the beast that grew them. A Seed too heavy for your Hold
will crack it — a **rupture**, which is exactly as bad as it sounds and is how most
ambitious young practitioners die.

### (b) DEEDS — expanding acreage
Acreage does not come from monsters. Acreage comes from **doing something the Hold has
to make room for.** Surviving what should have killed you. Keeping a promise that cost
you. Understanding a thing you had been refusing to understand. The Ledger's official
term is a **deed**; the vulgar term is *earning your dirt*.

This cannot be bought, farmed, or rushed, which is the single greatest source of
frustration in the practitioner world and the reason rich talentless heirs exist.

### (c) WORK — raising weight and coherence
Daily, boring, essential. **Drawing** (pulling ambient Wild in through the breath and
the skin) raises weight. **Walking your Hold** — going inside and physically crossing
your own terrain, tending it, arguing with it — raises coherence.

Everyone knows they should walk their Hold every day. Roughly as many people do it as
floss.

## 4. TERRAIN AND TECHNIQUE

**A technique is an export.** You cannot do a thing outside that is not true inside.

If your Hold contains a stand of iron-barked trees, you can put iron bark on your arm.
If it contains a stormfront, you can hand someone a lightning bolt. If it contains
forty acres of empty salt flat and nothing else, you can do absolutely nothing, and
the Ledger will grade you accordingly.

Techniques are named, registered, and traded like recipes. The Ledger keeps a
**Register of Workings** and charges a fee to search it. Most practitioners know four
to nine techniques. The good ones know three and have thought very hard about each.

**Combinations are where it gets interesting.** Terrain that borders other terrain
produces *edge effects* the source Seeds never had. A stormfront over a salt flat makes
something no stormfront alone can. Every serious practitioner is, secretly, a landscape
gardener.

### Mantle
At **Wold** rank, when the Verge first steps outside the skin, the Hold marks its owner.
This is the **Mantle** — a visible, permanent sign of the dominant terrain. Frost in the
hair. Eyes gone to riverstone. A voice with weather in it. Bark at the knuckles.

You do not choose your Mantle. Your Hold chooses it, and it is generally an unflattering
opinion about who you are.

## 5. THE SECOND AXIS — NOTION, LAW, WRIT

Past a point, more ground and more weight stop mattering. A country needs *rules*.

A **Law** is a single sentence that is true inside your Hold and, if you are strong
enough, becomes true outside it too, within your Verge. It is not a spell. It is a fact
you have decided to be responsible for.

Real registered Laws include:
- *Everything here falls toward me.*
- *Nothing crosses twice.*
- *The second cut is deeper.*
- *What is owed is paid.*
- *Cold does not stop at the skin.*

A Law must be **true of the holder** before it is true of the ground. This is why they
cannot be faked, purchased, or taught, and why an eighty-year-old Realm-rank can be
beaten by a young Marcher who happens to be more honest.

| Stage | Requirement | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Notion** | required to reach True Wold | a hunch. You can feel the shape of a rule you can't say yet. |
| **Law** | required to reach Realm | one sentence. Says itself. Holds inside your Verge. |
| **Writ** | required to reach Dominion | a Law that holds *outside* your Verge, on ground that isn't yours. |

## 6. THE LADDER

Ranks are conferred, measured, and recorded by the Ledger. They are not self-declared,
and claiming a rank you have not been assessed for is a crime with a specific fine
attached (four silver, plus costs).

| # | Rank | Threshold | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Unkindled** | — | interior shut. |
| 1 | **Ember** | Hold opens; a fire lit in it | You can *see* the Wild — currents, densities, pooling. Everything looks different forever. |
| 2 | **Hearth** | Hold walls hold under pressure | The body is remade to match the terrain. Faster, tougher, longer-lived (~110 yrs). First technique. |
| 3 | **Field** | the Hold gains a horizon | **Farsense**: you can feel other Holds — size, weight, mood — at a distance. The world becomes crowded. |
| 4 | **Wold** *(low / high / true)* | Verge steps outside the skin | You hold *ground*. **Mantle** appears. True Wold requires a **Notion**. Lifespan ~150. |
| 5 | **March** *(low / high / true)* | the Verge can be *moved and imposed* | You can push your country over someone else's. Duels become weather. Lifespan ~250. |
| 6 | **Realm** *(low / high / true)* | requires a **Law** | Your Law is true within your Verge. You are a local fact of nature. Lifespan ~600. |
| 7 | **Dominion** *(low / high / true)* | requires a **Writ** | Your Law travels. You change ground that isn't yours. Roughly thirty exist on the Sill. Lifespan: long. |
| 8 | **Sovereign** | the Hold becomes self-sustaining — it makes its own Wild | You no longer draw from the world; the world draws from you. Nine known. |
| 9 | **Firmament** | — | Not a rank. A place, and a rumour, and a threat. |

**Rank gaps are qualitative, not incremental.** A hundred Hearths cannot kill a Wold.
The Wold does not have a hundred times the power; the Wold has *territory*, and inside
territory the rules are different. This is the single most important fact about combat
on the Sill and every strategist's first lesson.

## 7. CRAFT

Seeds are not only for planting. **Grafters** cut, cure, and fit them:
- **Grafts** — a Seed fitted into a *body* rather than a Hold. Faster than planting,
  cruder, and it never quite becomes yours. Common among soldiers, frowned on by purists.
- **Charms** — a Seed set in a made object. A knife that remembers being cold. A lamp
  that burns without fuel. The Sill's entire luxury economy.
- **Wards** — terrain imposed on real ground and pinned there. Town walls. Sickrooms.
  Cellars. This is the civil engineering of the world.

Grafters are as respected as surgeons and paid rather better.

## 8. THE DEEP WILD AND THE WELLINGS

The Sill is the habitable middle of a three-layer world.

- **The Deep Wild** below: where the Wild wells up raw and terrain grows without anyone
  owning it. Monsters are not evil; they are *ecology*. Something has to live in a place
  that dense.
- **The Sill**: thin, fair, and rich ground. Where people live.
- **The Firmament** above: where the Wild is thin to nothing, and the things that live
  there had to become self-sustaining to do it. Sovereigns. Sky-holds. Silence.

A **Welling** is a place where the Deep comes up through the Sill. They are hunted,
farmed, licensed, taxed, and occasionally they win. A Welling left alone long enough
produces a **surge** — terrain and its inhabitants spilling out across fair ground,
which is how villages stop existing.

Wellings are the Sill's dungeons, mines, gold rushes, and disasters, all at once.

## 9. HARD RULES (do not break without a written retcon in `canon/continuity-log.md`)

1. No pets, no bonded companions, no summoned creatures that fight for you. Terrain is
   not a friend. If something in a Hold seems to have opinions, that is *horror*, and
   it is a plot, not a build.
2. Rank cannot be faked to Farsense. You can hide *how much* you have; you cannot make
   a Hearth feel like a March.
3. Nobody gets stronger off-page. Every advancement is earned in text, with the deed
   named.
4. A Seed cannot be un-planted. Terrain is permanent. Bad builds are permanent.
5. Laws must cost the holder something. A Law you can put down is not a Law.
6. The Wild is not sentient, has no plan, and does not care. Anything that claims
   otherwise is lying or is something else wearing the Wild's name.
7. Power does not solve social problems. A Dominion can flatten a city and still cannot
   make a Ledger clerk process a form early.
