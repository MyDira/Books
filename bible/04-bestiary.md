# BESTIARY — THE WILDBORN
*Append-only. Every monster used in a chapter gets an entry the same session, with the
book/chapter of first appearance. Seeds must be consistent forever.*

## PRINCIPLES
Wildborn are **ecology, not evil.** Terrain that got dense enough to want things.
They are territorial, hungry, and — the higher you go — increasingly capable of
strategy, spite, and speech. They are not demons and they do not serve a dark lord.

**Grading** matches the human ladder: an Ember-grade beast, a Hearth-grade beast, and
so on. A Hearth-grade beast is *substantially* more dangerous than a Hearth-grade human,
because it does not have to sleep, negotiate, or think about anything else.

**Seed rule:** the Seed carries the beast's *dominant terrain*, not its abilities.
You do not get the wolf. You get the pines and the wind the wolf lived in.

## THE LOAM — recorded species

### Tallow-crawler — *Ember grade*
Pale, boneless, the length of a forearm. Nests in Welling seams. Moves like spilled
fat. Bite is not venomous, merely septic. **Seed:** pin-head size, greasy. Terrain: a
patch of warm damp ground that will not dry. Worth four copper and nobody's respect.
*First appearance: B01 c04.*

### Chalk-hound — *Hearth grade*
Downland pack hunter, bone-white, runs the barrows. Hide takes a blade badly. Hunts by
Farsense, so hiding does not work; it hunts the *Hold*, not the body.
**Seed:** knuckle-sized, chalky. Terrain: dry white downland, thin grass, good sightlines.
Common first plant for Loam practitioners; safe, cheap, unglamorous.
*First appearance: TBD.*

### Cinder-boar — *Hearth/low-Wold grade*
Six hundred pounds and permanently smouldering. Charges in a straight line and is very
bad at turning, which is the entire strategy for killing one.
**Seed:** fist-sized, hot to the touch for a week. Terrain: a scorched hollow, always
smoking, nothing grows. Aggressive plant — it burns its borders.
*First appearance: TBD.*

### Nine-in-a-coat — *Wold grade*
Nine tallow-crawlers that have grown together and learned to want a shape. Wears what
it has eaten. Loam folklore says it knocks. Loam folklore is correct.
**Seed:** nine small seeds fused. Terrain: unstable; ruptures inexperienced Holds.
*First appearance: TBD.*

### Frost-wolf — *Wold grade* (northern Loam / the Chalk in winter)
**Seed:** terrain — black pine, permanent wind, hard cold ground. One of the most-planted
terrains on the Sill because it borders well with almost everything.
