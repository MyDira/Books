# INSTITUTIONS OF THE SILL

## THE LEDGER
The Sill's spine. Not a government, not a guild, not a religion — an **accounting firm
that outlived every government that ever tried to audit it.**

Founded 812 years ago on one premise: *in a world where power is territory, somebody
has to write down who holds what, or there is no such thing as a border, a contract,
or a town.* Every state on the Sill accepts Ledger assessment because the alternative
is that every dispute is settled by whoever is heaviest.

**The Ledger does:**
- **Assessment** — measures Holds, confers rank, issues **tallies** (a stamped brass
  chit with your three numbers on it). Assessment is mandatory at kindling and voluntary
  thereafter, except it isn't, because nobody will hire you without a current tally.
- **The Register of Workings** — the catalogue of every named technique. Searchable
  for a fee. Registering a working of your own devising is the cheapest immortality
  available to a commoner.
- **Writs of Hunt** — monster contracts. Posted at every **Hall of Hunt**. Graded by
  rank, priced by weight, and the source of most young practitioners' income and most
  young practitioners' deaths.
- **The Assize** — the ranked trials. Held at provincial seats every four years, and at
  the Sill seat every twelve. Officially, an assessment event. Actually, the largest
  spectator sport in the world.
- **Welling licences** — who may enter, at what rank, and what percentage the Ledger takes.

**The Ledger does not:** rule, tax (except fees), keep an army (except it does, and it
is called the *Circuit*, and it is very small and very heavy), or admit to having
opinions.

**Culture:** forms, seals, appeal windows, precedence, a genuine and unironic love of
correct paperwork. Auditors are field agents: itinerant, eccentric, and — because a
lone clerk must be able to walk into a Dominion's house and demand to measure him —
quietly among the strongest people alive.

**Ranks within:** Clerk → Registrar → Assessor → Auditor → Auditor Emeritus →
the **Quorum** (nine, unnamed, allegedly).

## THE HALLS OF HUNT
Franchise operations, licensed by the Ledger, run locally. A board, a bar, a bath,
a bunkroom, a bonesetter, and a bulletin of who died this month. Every town has one.
The Hall is the series' recurring social hub — the tavern with an actuarial table.

## THE GREAT HOUSES
Seven in the Weft, more elsewhere. Multi-generational families who solved the acreage
problem the only way it can be solved dynastically: by arranging that their children
have very good *deeds* available to them at fourteen. Houses do not breed talent. They
**manufacture opportunity**, which works nearly as well and is far more resented.

## GRAFTERS' CONCORD
The craft trade. Cutting, curing, and fitting Seeds. Fiercely guild-ish, hereditary
recipes, a licensing exam with a 9% pass rate. They are the only people on the Sill
who can tell the Ledger to wait.

## THE QUIET (later-book seed)
Not a faction. A word people use for the places where the Wild has gone *out*, and for
whatever it is that takes it. Nobody has an explanation. The Ledger's official position
is that the Quiet does not exist, filed under a case number that has been open 400 years.
