# WILDHOLD — Series Premise
*The one-page pitch. If a cold session reads only one file, read this, then `canon/STATE.md`.*

---

## THE SERIES
**WILDHOLD** — 25 books of progression fantasy set on the Sill.

**Tone:** warm, forward-leaning, funny. Terrible things happen and the book does not
wallow. The default emotional temperature is *company on a hard road*. Bureaucratic
comedy is a load-bearing element, not a garnish.

**Engine:** hunt monsters → take Seeds → plant terrain → gain techniques → advance
rank → face something that makes your new rank look small. Repeat at ever-larger scale
until the scale is the whole world.

**Structure:** every book is a complete story with its own goal, antagonist, and
climax, ending on at least one earned advancement and one new question. No book ends
on an unresolved cliffhanger of the *"and then he fell off the cliff, buy book 2"*
variety. Books end on **satisfaction plus appetite**.

---

## THE PREMISE

Everyone on the Sill has a country inside them. At fourteen you light it, a Ledger
clerk measures it, and that measurement is more or less your life.

**Wick Alder** lights his and gets forty-one acres of cold salt flat under a starless
sky. No fire. No walls. Nothing growing. The clerk has to invent a notation for it,
because the form does not have a box for *this much ground and this little in it*.

Weight: 0.0. Coherence: ragged. Rank: **Ember, unrankable.**

He is, by every measure the Ledger keeps, the weakest kindled person in the province
and the largest. A normal Hold is a room, and a room can hold one fire and, eventually,
a few careful things. Wick has forty-one acres and no idea what to do with them, and
the only thing anyone can agree on is that he should not have got them.

What nobody points out, because nobody is looking at the problem from that side, is
that **acreage is the number you cannot buy, cannot farm, and cannot rush**, and Wick
Alder was handed a lifetime's worth of it at fourteen with nothing to put in it.

He just has to fill it. One monster at a time. Starting from zero, in a world where
zero gets you killed.

## THE CAST SHAPE
*(Cradle/Path-of-Ascension style: the crew accretes, and once someone is in, they stay in.)*

- **Wick Alder** — all ground, no weight. Patient, stubborn, relentlessly curious,
  bad at being told no. The opposite of a prodigy: he is a *compounding asset*.
- **Nettle** (Annet Faring) — the inverse problem. A Hold two feet across and denser
  than an anvil. Hits like the end of the world, cannot grow, and is furious about it.
  Their arcs are keys to each other's locks; neither notices for a long time.
- **Auditor Emeritus Ossian Pell** — an itinerant Ledger auditor in an ink-stained coat
  who is delighted by everything, insists on his full and absurd job title, and is
  obviously several ranks above anything he admits to. Has an agenda. It is not a
  cruel one, which is somehow worse.
- **The crew** — accretes over books 1–8 and never fully departs. A grafter, a
  registrar, a disgraced heir, a monster-ecologist, a cook who matters. Assembled by
  need and kept by loyalty.

---

## WHAT THIS SERIES IS NOT
- Not grimdark. Not a harem. Not a pet/companion/summon story (**hard rule**).
- Not a game-overlay LitRPG — no floating blue boxes. The "interface" is the **Ledger**:
  an actual bureaucracy that measures, grades, ranks, licenses, and files. All the
  number-satisfaction, zero of the video-game seams.
- Not a story where the protagonist wins by being told he is special.

## THE LONG SHAPE
Village → province → the Assize → the Deep Wild → the continent → the Firmament →
