# THE SILL — Geography
*Expand as the series travels. Never contradict; only add. Anything invented in a
chapter must be recorded here the same session.*

## COSMOLOGY
Three layers, stacked:
- **The Deep Wild** — below. Not underground exactly; *under* in the way a riverbed is
  under a river. Reached through Wellings. Terrain grows there unowned and unowned
  terrain grows teeth.
- **The Sill** — the habitable middle. Everything below is called "the Deep," everything
  above "the Firmament," and everything here is called "home" or "this dump" depending.
- **The Firmament** — above. Thin Wild, thinning to none. Only self-sustaining Holds
  survive there. Sovereigns live there. It is visible on clear nights as a faint
  fixed lattice people call **the Rafters**.

## THE SILL — Political Shape (as known at series start)
The Sill is a continent-sized shelf. Charted regions, west to east:

### The Loam (starting region — Books 1–3)
Fair-to-thin farm country. Barley, sheep, root vegetables, absolutely nothing else.
Nine market towns, four hundred villages, one Ledger circuit that visits twice a year
and is always late.
- **Hollow Alder** — Wick's village. Pop. ~300. Thin ground (0.35), which is why the
  kindlings there are famously poor and the barley is famously bad. Named for a stand
  of hollow alders by the ford.
- **Marrowfield** — market town, three days' walk. Pop. ~4,000. Has a permanent Ledger
  office, a Grafter, a Hall of Hunt, and a genuine paved street.
- **The Chalk** — a band of white downland north of Marrowfield. Thin ground, old bones,
  a great many barrows nobody opens twice.
- **Tallow Welling** — a small, licensed, thoroughly farmed Welling two hours from
  Marrowfield. Ember-to-Hearth grade. The training wheels of the province.

### The Weft (Books 3–6)
River country. Rich ground. Barge cities, floating markets, seven Great Houses who
have been quietly at war for two centuries via lawsuits.
- **Sennet-on-Weft** — the provincial seat. Where the **Assize** is held.

### The Rakes (later)
Broken highland. Wellings everywhere. Effectively lawless; nominally licensed.

### Further (unwritten, reserve for books 8+)
- **The Girdle** — the equatorial band of permanent surge.
- **The Quiet Coast** — where the Ledger's authority stops.
- **Vantage** — the city under the Rafters. Dominion country.

## UNITS (locked — all geometry in the series checks against these)
- **1 pace = 1 yard.** Surveyors in the Loam have been grateful for this since Y-200.
- **1 acre = 4,840 square paces.**
- **1 barley-field = 0.6 acre** — Hollow Alder's unit; what a man can work in a day.
  Wick thinks in these and always will.
- A circle *n* paces across is about **3.14 × n** paces round. Wick does this arithmetic
  constantly and on the page, so it must always be right.

## DISTANCES & TRAVEL
Walking: 20 miles/day. Cart: 25. River barge: 40. There is no fast travel below March
rank, and travel time is a *feature* of the series, not an obstacle to route around —
roads are where the crew becomes a crew.

## THE CALENDAR
Twelve months, 30 days each, plus five loose days at midwinter called **the Slack**,
which belong to nobody and are therefore when everything interesting happens.
Months: Thaw, Seed, Green, Long, High, Dust, Gather, Turn, Rust, Dark, Deep, Still.
Year 1 of the current reckoning = the founding of the Ledger. Series opens **Y-812**.
