# STYLE GUIDE — WILDHOLD
*Read before writing any chapter. This is the voice contract.*

## POV & TENSE
Third person limited, past tense. **Wick is the default POV.** Other POVs are allowed
but must be (a) a named section break, (b) no more than ~25% of a book, and (c) never
used to hide information the reader would resent losing.

## THE VOICE
Warm, dry, forward-moving. The narration is on the characters' side and finds them
funny. Sentences are mostly short. Paragraphs are mostly short. The prose gets out of
the way of the story and then, once or twice a chapter, does something lovely and
gets out of the way again.

**Model the rhythm on:** a friend telling you what happened, who is a very good
storyteller and slightly enjoys making you wait.

### Do
- Concrete nouns. Barley, brass, blood, boot-leather.
- Comedy from *character and system*, not from jokes. The Ledger's forms are funny
  because they are sincere.
- Let people be competent. Competence is the primary pleasure of this genre.
- Land every chapter on a turn — a decision, a reveal, a cost, or a door opening.
- Physical stakes rendered physically. Exhaustion, cold, the specific ache of a Hold
  running empty.

### Don't
- No purple. If a sentence has three adjectives, remove two.
- No grimdark. Bad things happen; the camera does not linger and the survivors make
  jokes.
- **No em-dash-heavy narration.** Use them, but sparingly and deliberately.
- No prophecy, no chosen one, no "he was destined."
- Never explain a system rule twice. Trust the reader; put the reminder in the *action*.
- No cliffhanger chapter endings more than one in four. Overuse is cheap and readers
  in this genre notice.

## ACTION SCENES — the house method
The genre lives or dies here. Every significant fight must have:
1. **A problem statement.** Not "he was stronger." *What specifically* about this
   opponent breaks Wick's usual answer?
2. **Terrain.** Both the real ground and both fighters' Holds. Fights are geography.
3. **A cost paid on-page.** Wild spent, ground lost, a technique burned out, a bone.
4. **An adaptation.** He tries the thing. The thing fails. He learns something *about
   the system* and tries a new thing. This is the beat readers came for.
5. **A clean resolution the reader can diagram.** If you cannot draw it, rewrite it.

Fights are 800–2,000 words. Longer only for book climaxes (up to 5,000, split across
chapters).

## THE NUMBERS
The reader is here for progression. Deliver it **explicitly and often**:
- Ledger assessments printed as small, clean blocks (see format below).
- Characters saying real numbers out loud. "Four acres. Weight nine." Not "he had grown
  much stronger."
- A visible ratchet at least once every three chapters: a Seed planted, a technique
  learned, a coherence grade improved, a rank taken.

### Ledger assessment block format
Use verbatim, indented as a blockquote:

> **LEDGER OF THE SILL — ASSESSMENT**
> Subject: Wick Alder, of Hollow Alder, the Loam
> Acreage: 41.2 · Weight: 0.0 · Coherence: ragged
> Terrain: none recorded
> Workings: none registered
> **Rank: Ember (unrankable)**
> Assessor: R. Camber, Registrar, Third Circuit

Use sparingly — 1–3 per book, at genuine milestones. They are punctuation, not wallpaper.

## CHAPTER SHAPE
2,200–3,200 words. Open in motion or in a question. Close on a turn.
No chapter is only travel, only exposition, or only feelings.
Every chapter must contain **at least one of**: progress, cost, revelation, or a
relationship changing state. Two is better.

## DIALOGUE
People interrupt, deflect, and talk past each other. Nobody explains the plot to
somebody who already knows it. Nettle is short and rude and precise. Pell talks in
complete paragraphs and answers a different question than the one asked. Wick asks
too many questions and does not notice he is doing it.

## RUNNING GAGS (maintain; add to `canon/threads.md` when you invent one)
- Pell's full title, which gets longer.
- Forms. Always the forms.
- Wick calculating acreage in barley-fields because it is the only unit he grew up with.
- Nobody can agree on the correct plural of "Wildborn."

## CONTINUITY DISCIPLINE
Every proper noun, technique, terrain, or rule you invent mid-chapter goes into
`canon/glossary.md` and `canon/continuity-log.md` **in the same work session**. No
exceptions. This is the single rule that makes 25 books possible.
