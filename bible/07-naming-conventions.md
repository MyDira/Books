# NAMING CONVENTIONS
*Consistency here is 80% of a world feeling real across 25 books.*

## PEOPLE
- **The Loam (farm country):** plain Anglo-Saxon. One or two syllables. Occupational or
  natural surnames. *Wick Alder, Annet Faring, Rab Camber, Sil Thatcher, Ma Dunnock.*
  Nicknames are common and stick harder than given names.
- **The Weft (river houses):** longer, softer, vowel-rich, often with a house particle
  *of* or *sen*. *Ilaria sen Vance, Corvel of House Marrow, Ossian Pell* (Pell is Weft-born).
- **The Rakes (highland):** hard consonants, no surnames — a byname earned. *Ket Ninefinger,
  Bar the Ash, Drum.*
- **Ledger officials:** always introduced with rank first. *Registrar Camber. Auditor Pell.*

## PLACES
Compound English-agricultural. *Hollow Alder, Marrowfield, Tallow Welling, Sennet-on-Weft,
the Chalk, the Rakes, the Girdle.* Avoid apostrophes. Avoid "-ia", "-dor", "-heim".

## WILDBORN (monsters)
`[quality]-[animal]` or a plain descriptive folk name. *Frost-wolf, cinder-boar,
river-drake, chalk-hound, the Sitting Man, tallow-crawlers, nine-in-a-coat.*
Higher-rank Wildborn get **names**, not descriptions, and the shift is deliberate and
ominous.

## TECHNIQUES (Workings)
Two to four plain words, no fantasy grammar. Registered names are slightly pompous
because a clerk wrote them down; the common name is blunt.
- Registered: *the Alder Standing Working.* Common: *"the anchor."*
- Registered: *Second Winter.* Common: *"the cold one."*

## TERRAIN
Named as landscape, not as powers. Not "Frost Domain" — **"the black pines"** or
**"the salt flat"** or **"the burn."** Characters refer to their own abilities by
geography, which is one of the world's most distinctive textures. Preserve it.

## LAWS
One plain declarative sentence, always italicised in text, always eight words or fewer.
*Nothing crosses twice. What is owed is paid. The ground remembers.*

## FORBIDDEN
No apostrophe-names (K'thal). No "Grand/Supreme/Ultimate" prefixes. No word ending in
"-mancer." No character named with a Q or X unless it is a Weft house and I have a
reason.
