# THE LONG ARC — loose, private, revisable
*Not a plan the reader ever sees. A gravity well the series falls toward. The
story-architect agent may revise this at book boundaries; nothing here is binding
until it has been paid for on the page.*

## THE QUESTION UNDER THE SERIES
Why does the Sill exist as a *shelf* — a thin habitable band between a Wild that wells
up and a Firmament where it has gone out? Worlds do not naturally come with a middle.

Working answer (revisable, unrevealed): **the Sill is a managed depth.** Something,
long ago, decided how much Wild a world should have at the level where people live,
and built a mechanism to keep it there. The Wellings are not wounds. They are *valves*.
The Quiet is not a disease. It is *the other half of the valve*, and it has begun
operating on a schedule nobody remembers setting.

## MACRO-MOVEMENTS (each ~5 books, elastic)
1. **Books 1–5 · THE LOAM AND THE WEFT.** Wick fills the first of his ground; the crew
   forms; the Assize. Scope: a province. Question: *can he ever be worth anything?*
2. **Books 6–10 · THE DEEP.** Down. Wellings as places rather than dungeons. What lives
   at the bottom, and why it is organised. Scope: a continent. Question: *what are the
   monsters for?*
3. **Books 11–15 · THE LAW.** Realm rank; the politics of people who are facts of
   nature; the Ledger's own rot; the Quorum. Scope: the Sill. Question: *who decides
   what is true?*
4. **Books 16–20 · THE FIRMAMENT.** Up. Sovereigns. The Rafters are a machine. Scope:
   the world. Question: *who built the shelf?*
5. **Books 21–25 · THE QUIET.** The valve closing. The cost of keeping a world at a
   habitable depth. Scope: everything. Question: *what is a world worth, and who pays?*

## THE ENDGAME SHAPE
Wick's forty-one acres are not a fluke and not a gift. They are a *measurement error* —
he was kindled on ground so thin that his Hold took its size from something other than
the local Wild. Late series, the reader learns what. It should feel inevitable and
should not diminish the work he did.

**The final Law must be one he could have said in Book 1 and would not have meant.**

## CONSTRAINTS ON THE ENDGAME
- No retcon of any advancement. Everything earned stays earned.
- The reveal must make *rereading Book 1* better, not embarrassing.
- The series ends with the crew intact enough that a reader is glad, and changed enough
  that it cost something.
