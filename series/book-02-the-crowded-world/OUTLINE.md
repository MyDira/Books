# Book 02 — THE CROWDED WORLD
*Draft outline v1 — pending story-architect pass.*

**Logline:** Wick holds a rank he cannot back up, in a town where everyone can read it off
his tally — and the sister who stopped writing has found the one answer to being capped that
he and Nettle have both refused.

**New system idea:** *Overfilled ground spreads on its own.* Terrain settles outward from its
own borders, so the fastest way to fill a country is not to plant more — it is to **make more
edges**. This is the answer to Pell's "find out what makes a thing settle," and it turns
forty-four acres from nine hundred years of hunting into a landscape-gardening problem.

**Want (concrete, checkable):** **Find Bri Alder.** She is in Marrowfield, she has sent money
and no word three times, and Gran said *find your sister* on a road in Dust and he did not
look. Checkable: either he is standing in front of her or he is not.

**Second hand on the clock:** *held on the boundary*. Every writ one grade down at one grade's
pay; no sponsorship; re-testable at his own expense; **challengeable in public by anyone of
his rank or above.** It costs him on the page, weekly, in money and in standing, and the only
way out is weight — 1.5, into the Hearth band, held **in full**.

**Antagonist (the argument):** a Grafter. Warm, licensed, extremely good, and **right**:
*a graft is the only real answer to being capped, and refusing one is a luxury paid for by
somebody else.* Nettle refuses on principle. Bri could not afford principle. And Bri is
stronger than either of them and hates it, and that is the argument the whole book has to
survive.

**Antagonist (the pressure):** Marrowfield itself — four thousand people, two thousand posts
in the river, and **Farsense arriving**, which turns a town into a crowd he cannot switch off.

**Setting:** Marrowfield. Its Hall, its Ledger office, its Concord workshops, and the Tallow
Welling two hours beyond it — licensed, fenced, farmed, watched, and nothing like the hole
under the ford.

## ACT STRUCTURE (32 chapters, target ~80,000 words)
- **Act I — ch 1–8 · ONE GRADE DOWN.** Open on a hunt that makes "the wet" and "the crust"
  look clever inside ten pages *(carried forward from the B01 architect pass)*. Then
  Marrowfield, and the tally, and the weekly humiliation of the status. He starts looking for
  Bri and finds the shape of a hole where she should be.
- **Turn 1 — ch 8:** he finds her, and she is not in trouble, and that is worse.
- **Act II-a — ch 9–16 · WHAT IT COSTS TO BE ANSWERED.** The Grafter. The argument, made
  properly and won by the other side. Nettle and Bri in a room. Meanwhile Wick, chasing
  weight, discovers the thing he was not looking for: **his warm damp has grown, and he did
  not plant it.**
- **MIDPOINT — ch 16 · THE REVERSAL:** he has spent the whole book (and all of Book 1) buying
  ground by the Seed. The ground has been *making itself* at the edges the entire time, at a
  rate he could have measured any night since Long, and he never looked because he was
  looking at the middle. **Nine hundred years becomes a number he can change.** And it
  reframes his six survey pegs from instruments into **seed points**.
- **Act II-b — ch 17–25 · EDGES.** The gardening. Registering the workings. The challenge —
  somebody of rank calls his assessment in public, and he cannot win it on weight. Bri's
  situation turns from a moral question into a practical one.
- **Turn 2 — ch 25 · LOW POINT:** the thing about edges that is also true of people.
- **Act III — ch 26–32 · THE CROWDED WORLD.** Tallow Welling. Field arrives as a **cost**:
  Farsense in a town of four thousand, and he cannot turn it off, and the first thing he
  feels with it is what Bri actually is.
