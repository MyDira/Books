# Book 02 — THE CROWDED WORLD
*v2 — revised against the Book 1 architect criteria before drafting.*

**Logline:** Wick holds a rank he cannot back up, in a town where anyone can read that off his
tally — and the sister who stopped writing has found the one answer to being capped that he
and Nettle have both refused.

**New system idea:** *Overfilled ground spreads on its own.* Terrain settles outward from its
own borders, so the fastest way to fill a country is not to plant more — it is to **make more
edges.** This is the answer to Pell's "find out what makes a thing settle."

**Want (concrete, checkable):** **Find Bri Alder.** Either he is standing in front of her or
he is not. Gran said *find your sister* on a road in Dust and he did not look.

**Second hand on the clock:** *held on the boundary*. Writs one grade down at one grade's pay;
no sponsorship either way; re-testable at his own expense; **publicly challengeable.** It must
cost him on the page, weekly, in money and standing.

## THE FIVE QUESTIONS — answered

**1 · What carries chapters 9–32 once Bri is found at c8?**
Not the search. **The argument.** Finding her opens the real problem, and it is the same
question Wick faces about his own forty-four acres: *what will you spend of yourself to stop
being small?* Bri answered it. Nettle refused to. Wick has refused twice and has not yet paid
for it. That runs to the last page.

**2 · The antagonist is an argument, and I do not get to win it.**
The **Grafter** — warm, licensed, twenty-two years, genuinely good at the work — says:
*a graft is the only real answer to being capped, and refusing one is a luxury somebody else
pays for.* **Bri is right about the world. Nettle is right about herself. The book must not
resolve it.** If I catch myself building the Grafter to be knocked down, the chapter is wrong.
Bri must be **stronger than either of them, and better off, and not sorry** — and something is
still missing, and she will not say it is the graft, and she may be telling the truth.

**3 · THE MIDPOINT (c16) — his position gets WORSE.**
He discovers that overfilled ground spreads on its own at its edges. Nine hundred years
becomes a number he can change — *and in the same breath he understands what the seam is.*
**The seam is not an invader. It is the same process, running on ground he does not own.**
It is made *entirely of edge*, so it spreads faster than anything he can grow, and the
deceleration he was so relieved by at the end of Book 1 is not weakness. **It has been
building length, and length is what it grows from.** Every single thing he learns about
making his country live is also a description of the thing eating it, and he cannot fight it
without unlearning the one method that makes him work.

**4 · Farsense is a cost before it is a tool — the mechanism.**
At Field he cannot stop feeling every kindled person within a few hundred paces as a weight
and a shape. In Marrowfield that is **two thousand posts standing in a river.** He cannot
sleep in a town. He cannot cross a market. It is not a mood: it is a permanent change to what
being in a room is.
- **What it lets him learn that nothing else could:** he can feel a **graft from the outside**
  — a working sitting *next to* a person instead of in them, like a coat on a chair. He feels
  it on Bri before she tells him. He cannot un-feel it. **She knows he can feel it.**
- **The permanent cost:** he will never again meet a person without knowing roughly what they
  are. A certain kind of ordinary friendliness ends for ever, and Gran notices first.

**5 · The one scene where "on the boundary" ruins something: HE LOSES A CHALLENGE.**
Public, at the Hall, called by somebody entitled to call it. He cannot win it — a re-test
measures weight and he has none — and he **loses**, on the page, in front of the town, and
the loss stands. That is the low point and it is not overturned by a clever provision. This
book does not get to use the same trick twice.

## ACT STRUCTURE (32 chapters, ~80,000 words)
- **Act I — ch 1–8 · ONE GRADE DOWN.** Open on a hunt that makes "the wet" and "the crust"
  look clever inside ten pages. Marrowfield. The tally read aloud by strangers. He starts
  looking for Bri and finds the shape of a hole where she should be.
- **Turn 1 — ch 8:** he finds her. She is not in trouble. That is worse.
- **Act II-a — ch 9–15 · THE ANSWER HE REFUSED.** The Grafter. Nettle and Bri in a room.
  Wick, chasing weight, finds the thing he was not looking for: his warm damp has grown, and
  he did not plant it.
- **MIDPOINT — ch 16 · THE GOOD NEWS IS THE BAD NEWS.** (See Q3.)
- **Act II-b — ch 17–25 · EDGES.** The gardening. The Register. The challenge. Bri's situation
  turns from a moral question into a practical one.
- **Turn 2 — ch 25 · LOW POINT: he loses the challenge in public**, and the thing about edges
  that is also true of people.
- **Act III — ch 26–32 · THE CROWDED WORLD.** Tallow Welling. Field at ~c29, arriving as a
  cost. The last three chapters are what it is like to be him now.
