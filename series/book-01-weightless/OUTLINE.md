# Book 01 — WEIGHTLESS
*v2 — revised after architect pass. See AUDIT.md for the full pass.*

**Logline:** A boy whose Hold opens forty-one acres wide and completely empty has six
months to be worth measuring — and discovers that the one thing making him remarkable is
the one thing making the rank impossible.

**New system idea:** Hearth is conferred when a Hold's walls hold under pressure. Forty-one
acres of open salt flat has no walls. *Filling is not enclosing.*

**Want:** Be assessed at **Hearth** by the Rust circuit, or be recorded **Failed of
Kindling** — permanent, appealable in theory, never overturned in practice. Green → Rust.
Six months.
**Second hand on the clock:** the Whitts tenancy. Rent falls at Gather. The barley will
not cover it. This is the weekly pressure the deadline cannot supply.

**Antagonist (the argument):** **Dorn Vance**, high Wold, holds the Loam licence.
His real claim is not *unlicensed hunting is unsafe*. It is: **"Failed of Kindling is
survivable and dying is not. You have confused a bureaucratic category with a death
sentence, and you would rather die than be ordinary."** He is right. That indicts Wick's
character, not his caution.
His insufficiency is **structural, not personal**: correct procedure for a Wold-grade
beast is to seal the Welling and wait for a Circuit officer with authority to authorise
a rank-appropriate hunt. **Eleven days.** The Nine's schedule is **six**. He is right on
every count except the calendar.
Present from ch 6 (rumour, the licence as an economic fact) and physically in the Welling
before Wick gets there — a stake driven, a form nailed to a tree.

**Antagonist (the monster):** the **Nine-in-a-Coat**. Nine tallow-crawlers grown together
into one thing that wants a shape, and wears what it has eaten. Wold grade. Loam folklore
says it knocks. The inverse of Wick: many things that became one, against one large thing
made of nothing.

**Setting:** Hollow Alder and the unlicensed Welling under the ford. Marrowfield in the
back third. Thin ground throughout. A book about scarcity.

## ACT STRUCTURE (32 chapters)
- **Act I — ch 1–8 · THE MEASUREMENT.** Thin ground. The survey. Form 9-C. Forty-one
  acres of nothing. The kindness, which is worse than cruelty. He finds the seam.
- **Turn 1 — ch 8:** an unlicensed Welling under the ford. He does not report it, and he
  knows exactly what that makes him.
- **Act II-a — ch 9–16 · THE WRONG WORK.** Hunting tallow-crawlers alone with a spear.
  The first Seed, worth four copper and no respect, and the best thing that ever happened
  to him. Nettle. Pell. Dorn's tally, printed, as a yardstick.
- **MIDPOINT — ch 16 · THE REVERSAL.** He watches a rupture. A Hold under pressure fails
  at its weakest boundary — and Wick does the arithmetic out loud and says the ugly
  sentence himself: *Hearth needs walls. I don't have walls. Everything I have been doing
  is the wrong work.* His position gets **worse** at 50%. New driving question for Act
  II-b: **how do you put walls around forty-one acres?** *(Deed 1 — acreage.)*
- **Act II-b — ch 17–25 · ENCLOSURE.** Failed experiments, each countable. The white down.
  First non-zero public weight reading. Salt/down edge effect. Registration. Dorn's offer.
  The Welling is on a schedule. Something in it is organising.
- **Turn 2 — ch 24–26 · LOW POINT.** The knocking starts. Someone is taken. Dorn offers
  Wick a licensed beater's place under his own writ — legal, paid, safe, stops the
  striking-off. Terms: **the sponsor holds the Seeds.** Wick lives, and is small, forever.
  He refuses. Dorn strikes him off for unlicensed hunting — a *separate offence* — and is
  correct to. *(Deed 2 — acreage.)* Gran is present. Gran is why he can refuse.
- **Act III — ch 27–32 · THE COAT.** Dorn goes in by the book; eleven days is not six.
  Into the Welling. Strength fails at ch 29. At ch 30 the answer is the terrain he has had
  since chapter three and has never once used: **the way to kill a thing that wants a
  shape is to give it a place with no shape in it.** The Verge stops at the skin, so he
  must touch it and export — which is why he has to close to arm's reach on a Wold-grade
  beast and hold on. *(Deed 3 — acreage.)*
- **Landing — ch 31–32:** The Seed that should rupture him and does not, because there is
  nothing in him to crack. It lands forty acres out and starts propagating inward.
  The striking-off is not overturned — **a different form applies** (necessity/salvage),
  and **Dorn signs it**. A real tally with real numbers. And the last page inverts his
  single advantage: the usable acreage is shrinking, and there is a form for that too.
