# BOOK SUMMARIES
*What future books read instead of rereading. 10–15 lines per book, written at
`close-book`. Include: what happened, who joined, what Wick gained, what changed in the
world, what was left open.*

---
## BOOK 1 — *WEIGHTLESS* (32 ch · ~72,500 words · Y-812, Green 6 → Rust 4)

**What happened.** Wick Alder, fourteen, of Whitts strip in Hollow Alder (thin ground, 3–4
knots), surveys his family's whole tenancy by candle and string to find the thickest ground
to kindle on, and finds an *edge* in the Wild at the ford. He has himself kindled there
under Form 9-C and opens **41.2 acres of empty salt flat with no walls and no fire** —
weight 0.0, *unrankable*. Registrar Camber crosses out the word FEET on the form and writes
a note saying the defect is in the schedule and not in the subject; that note brings
**Auditor Ossian Pell**, who is auditing acreage irregularities and for whom Wick is the
sixth entry over one acre in eleven million assessments.

Under the ford is an unlicensed **Welling**. Wick hunts it illegally through the summer,
learns to kill tallow-crawlers with a horseshoe of sheep-salt laid against a nine-foot patch
of unowned **white grass**, and plants his first terrain — **the warm damp** — at the middle,
because the middle is the only place in forty-one acres he can find. He discovers that
**terrain carries weight without a fire**, and gets a rate: eleven breaths, then fifty-two.

He reports the Welling at Marrowfield and walks out before answering whether he has taken
Seed. He meets **Nettle** (0.002 acres, weight 7.4, coherence true — the opposite deficit)
and **Dorn Vance** (high Wold, holds the Loam licence), who confesses him, fines him, and
makes the book's counter-argument: *Failed of Kindling is survivable and being eaten is not.*

**MIDPOINT:** Ock ruptures in the lane on a bought Seed, and Pell quotes the Hearth standard
— *the subject's boundary sustains applied load without deformation.* Wick has no boundary.
Everything he has done all summer was the wrong work.

He surveys his own interior with six identical chalk-hound Seeds as pegs (9/7/5/3/2/1½ paces
at 100/400/1,000/3,000/8,000/20,000) and finds **there is no edge; it thins forever.** Gran:
*you don't wall the sky, you wall four acres of it.* He builds a rim round his nine paces by
**walking it, twenty-one nights, never crossing twice in the same place.**

The Ledger's correct procedure takes twenty-six days, not eleven; the nine-pound writ sits
unclaimed eight days because the five people who could take it are busy, old, frightened, or
barred. Dorn goes down alone under an abatement of imminent hazard, and is held. Wick refuses
Dorn's sponsorship (which would have paid the rent, remitted the fine and prevented the
striking-off) because **the sponsor holds the Seeds for seven years**, and tells Gran the
cost first and the reason second. Dorn lays a restraint to stop him. Wick crosses the chain
anyway, in contempt, at 4:04 p.m. on Gather 15.

**CLIMAX:** the **nine-in-a-coat** cannot be cut, walled, or salted — it carries the white
grass as a bridge. Wick takes hold of it and **carries it into his own Hold like a Seed**,
because forty-three acres of open salt has nothing to crack, and because his country is made
of the one substance a tallow-crawler cannot cross. The rim takes the load and holds. The
agreement fails in twenty minutes.

**Ending state.** **Hearth.** 43.9 acres · weight 0.6 · ragged (second class) · settled
0.0014 of 43.9 · two unregistered workings ("the wet", "the crust"). £8 12s 1d paid out; the
rent cleared; £4 4s 1d put by. The four-silver fine stands, and the contempt stays on the
book for ever (Dorn signed the abatement and entered the contempt *not proceeded with*, with
two pages of reasons).

**Who joined.** Nettle (bound by what they did and did not report; right arm grey to the
shoulder, recovering slowly). Pell (attached, cheerful, unexplained). Dorn (a debt, not an
enemy — and he will now have to hire Wick at rates, "like a person," and is annoyed).

**What changed in the world.** The village survived because a boy with no rank walked a road
with a sack of salt. Ock's father is dead; Ock is cracked and finished. Hollow Alder salts
its thresholds now, and so, shortly, will four hundred villages.

**Left open.** (1) **The seam** — the Nine's Seed planted itself in the salt and propagates
inward at about half a pace a night; it was 15 paces from the middle and is 11¼; ground it
crosses **cannot be planted**. (2) Why the white grass was **nine feet by nine feet**, which
is what a room is. (3) Why an unlicensed Welling opened under a village on 0.35 ground —
and why the shaft has a **draught**. (4) What Pell is really auditing. (5) Nettle's cap, and
her new question: what is a tiny, dense, perfectly coherent Hold *for*, as a design?
(6) Bri Alder, in Marrowfield, sending money and no word.
