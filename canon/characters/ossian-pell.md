# AUDITOR EMERITUS OSSIAN PELL
*The mentor. Delighted, ink-stained, and several ranks above anything he admits to.*

## FACTS
- Weft-born. Age unknown, appears fifty, is not. Long coat with ink on both cuffs and a
  satchel of forms that never runs out.
- Full title, which he insists on and which gets longer through the series:
  **"Deputy Under-Auditor Emeritus of the Third Circuit, Provisional, Seconded."**
  The demotions are real. He finds them hilarious. Nobody knows why he accepted them.
- Carries no weapon. Has never been seen to hurry.

## THE RULE OF PELL
He never lies and he never answers the question asked. He answers a *better* question,
usually one the asker will not understand for several books. When he does answer
directly it should be genuinely alarming.

## VOICE
Complete paragraphs. Enormous good cheer. Treats appalling danger and incorrect
paperwork as roughly equivalent problems, and is slightly more upset by the paperwork.
Asks people what they think before he tells them anything, and actually listens.

## AGENDA
He is auditing something. It is not the Welling. He attaches himself to Wick for a
reason he does not state and which is **not** "I sensed his hidden potential" — it must
be something specific, mundane on its face, and true. *(Working answer, revisable: Pell
is auditing an anomaly in acreage figures across the thin ground of the Loam. Wick is a
data point that should not exist. Pell wants to know why, and he is willing to spend
years finding out. He genuinely likes the boy, which complicates it.)*

## RANK
Actual: withhold. Reveal no earlier than Book 4, and make the reveal a *cost*, not a
flex. Whatever it is, it must explain why he is on a rural circuit and why that is a
punishment or a hiding place.

## HARD LIMIT
Pell must never solve a problem Wick could have solved. When Pell acts, the story pays
for it. Mentors who fix things kill progression fantasy.
