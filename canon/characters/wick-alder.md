# WICK ALDER
*POV character. Series protagonist.*

## FACTS (immutable once written)
- Age 14 at series open (Y-812). Born Y-798, month of Still.
- Hollow Alder, the Loam. Tenant farming family.
- Brown hair that will not lie down, sunburn across the nose, hands already farm-hard.
  Tall for the Loam and thin with it. Nothing striking about him and he knows it.
- Named **Wick** by his grandmother, who said a wick is the cheapest part of a candle
  and the only part that matters. She meant it as hope. The village mostly heard a joke.
- Family: **Gran Alder** (Mereth), **Tam Alder** (father), **Bri Alder** (sister, 3 yrs
  older, kindled well, left for Marrowfield, has stopped writing). Mother died Y-806.
- Cannot read fluently at series start. Can do arithmetic in his head very fast, in
  *barley-fields* — the only unit Hollow Alder measures anything in. (0.6 acres each.)

## VOICE
Asks too many questions and does not notice he is doing it. Polite to the point of being
annoying. When frightened he gets *more* curious, not less, which is a survival trait
right up until it isn't. Does not make jokes; is often accidentally funny. Says "right"
as a full sentence when he has decided something.

## WANT vs NEED
- **Wants:** to be worth measuring.
- **Needs:** to stop measuring himself with somebody else's ruler. (Long arc — do not
  resolve before Book 6.)

## THE DEFINING TRAIT
He does not get discouraged in a way that stops him working. He gets discouraged and
keeps working, which reads as stubbornness and is actually a kind of patience most
people cannot afford. **This is the engine of the whole series.** Everything he becomes
is compound interest.

## THE HOLD
Forty-one acres of flat salt pan. No fire. No walls. Sky with no stars in it. Cold in a
dry way. It is silent, and the silence is the part that frightens him — every other Hold
he has heard described has a fire in it.

Direction inside it is meaningful: he arrives at *the middle*, and everything else is
out. Distance in a Hold is real distance; crossing his takes him an hour of walking he
does not have.
