# DORETH FARING
*Grafter. Nettle's mother. Bri's teacher. Introduced B02 c003.*

## FACTS
- Concord licence, **22 years**, third best Grafter in Marrowfield, and says all three in the
  flat voice used for reading out a distance.
- Enormous: a head taller than any woman in Hollow Alder, built across the shoulders like a smith.
- **Nine feet of daub, weight 0.9.** Unchanged in 22 years and never will change.
- **Four grafts** in a line up the inside of the left forearm: chalk-hound (hands), river-drake
  (heat), **nine-in-a-coat (£11 — lets her feel the seat of a fitting instead of guessing)**,
  tallow-crawler (put in herself at twenty, because she was frightened of her own hands shaking).
- 61 Fell Street. Four lamps over the bench, a tool rack with the spaces marked, three pans.

## THE ARGUMENT SHE IS
**"A graft is for a woman with nine feet of daub doing work a Wold couldn't do, and doing it for
twenty-two years, and being paid for it."** She concedes Nettle is *right* — it isn't hers, and
every time she uses her hands she is reading a letter somebody else wrote — and says the
concession changes nothing: *"I had a choice between something that was mine and nothing, and
nothing is not a thing you can keep."*

**THE BOOK MUST NOT WIN THIS ARGUMENT.** Bri is right about the world; Nettle is right about
herself; Doreth's closing line stands as the series' position:
*"I do not know which of them is right, and anybody who tells you they do is selling something."*

## VOICE
Flat, unsoftened, generous by *stating things plainly* rather than by comfort. Refuses to be
managed and refuses to manage. Says "Mm" — **and this collides with Gran's assigned token, so
give Doreth her own**: she goes and does something to a pan when she needs a moment, and that
is her tell.

## WITH NETTLE
Eleven months of four hundred yards. She helped carry the chest when Nettle moved out and has
never once asked her to come back, because asking would require her daughter to stop believing
a thing she still believes. She has never said Bri's name to Nettle — she would think it cruel,
and would think it so fast she would not notice she had decided.
