# UNDER-WARDEN COEL · REGISTRAR H. SARROW
*Two institutional men who are both right. Introduced B02 c009 and c005.*

## UNDER-WARDEN COEL — Grafters' Concord, Fell Street chapter
~50, neat, **no ink on either cuff** (Wick notices, having got used to Pell). Brass pin
shaped like a pair of tongs. Quotes Register entries from memory.

**His position:** *Confluence* is the Concord's word — in their schedule 211 years, and every
practitioner ever entered under it has been a licensed Grafter, because until B02 nobody had
imagined a person doing it **inside himself.** He cannot touch a man outside the charter and
says so; that is precisely the difficulty.

**What he offered and Wick refused:** indenture, seven years, £11 bond **waived and recorded
as waived**, journeyman in three, board and 40s a month, and **access to 211 years of
Confluence — ~9,000 entries, of which ~400 are failures written up properly and unpublished.**
*"That is what an indenture actually buys. It is not the tuition. It is the list of things
that have already killed people."*

**He does not defend the age rule.** He has believed it wrong for nine years, has written to
Sennet about Bri Alder twice, and has had the same two very polite replies Doreth had. He
argues for the bond anyway, because of a man in the Rakes who put a river-drake Seed into his
brother's spine in Y-804 out of love; the brother took four years to die and was in his right
mind throughout.

**Voice:** pleasant, exact, announces what he is going to ask for before he asks. Says the
unflattering half out loud. Never threatens, and says so.

**Live thread:** the Concord/Ledger jurisdiction question will run ~nine years and end either
in a new box on a form or a rule that only a licensed Grafter may enter class Confluence.
**Entries made before a rule stand. Entries after do not.** Coel argues Wick's side and will
not always win.

## REGISTRAR H. SARROW — Register of Workings, Marrowfield
~60. **Thirty-four years in that room.** Great-grandson of *the* Sarrow — the knotted
candle-line in the Ledger's field manual, section four — and has spent thirty-four years
being asked about it. Calls his great-grandfather a nuisance.

**411,000 entries.** Earliest Y-119, a working for keeping bees warm, still on the shelf,
unused in six hundred years. *"That is not the point of the Register."*

**He has protested a form nine times in thirty-four years and all nine went to the Quorum.**
He protested for Wick on the twenty-third of Seed, in a four-page memorandum, and it reached
Sennet inside a month.

**Voice:** serene, precise, delighted by an anomaly, and completely without deference. Sends
a boy out for tea when something interesting happens. Says "Now, wait" to people who were
not going to interrupt.

**His standing warning:** the Register is public — 2s and anybody may search it — and
**searches are logged and the searcher is never disclosed** (rule since Y-411, "the only
reason anybody files anything true"). Wick's name had been searched four times before he had
a working in it.
