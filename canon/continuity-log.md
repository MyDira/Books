## B01 c001 — "Thin Ground"  [Y-812, Green 6, morning→night]
- Wick Alder, 14, Hollow Alder, the Loam. Tenant farm called **Whitts strip**, 40 acres.
- **The Sarrow's line**: waxed string, 9 knots, evenly spaced. Belonged to Wick's mother.
  Method: candle on floor, lie flat, pay out string toward the lean; flame stands upright
  at the edge of measurable pull; count knots. Four measurements from four corners =
  "a number"; one = "a guess." It is in the Ledger's own field manual, section four.
- Cellar reads **4 knots** (was 7 last autumn). Farm averages 3–4. Village ground thin.
- Wild pools in low places like water. Thicker in old forest / river bends / battlefields.
- Barley is a hand's width short of Wick's knee on Green 6; should be past it.
- **Family:** Tam Alder (father, quiet, hands in pockets, can find water). Gran Alder =
  **Mereth Alder**, 71, unkindled. Mother dead (Y-806), taught Wick the string.
  **Bri Alder**, sister, 3 yrs older, kindled 11 feet BRICK — Camber said "well now" aloud.
  Left for Marrowfield ~3 months after. Wrote, then less, then stopped.
- Kindling: age 14, Circuit comes twice yearly (Green and Rust), brass frame, taper.
  Standard result: a room, 9 ft in a village, 12 typical, 18 if wealthy/rich ground.
  Hollow Alder gets 9 ft of daub.
- **The Wickwater** — river at the ford; named for the village, not the boy. Six old
  **hollow alders** on the near bank, split, "like a row of chimneys." Village named for them.
- Green 6: river cold enough to ache the hand in 30 seconds after a week of sun. Wrong.
- **The Rafters** visible on clear nights: faint fixed lattice past the stars, horizon to
  horizon, too regular to be natural. Village position: not their business.
- Wick can't read fluently. Does arithmetic fast, in **barley-fields** (0.6 acre each);
  Whitts = 66 barley-fields.
- Gran named him Wick: "a wick is the cheapest part of a candle and the only part that
  matters." Meant as hope; village heard a joke.
- **Turn:** Wick surveys the whole tenancy by candle at night to find the thickest ground
  to kindle on. First square: 3.

## B01 c002 — "Form Nine-C"  [Green 6–9]
- Survey complete: 66 squares, 264 measurements, on the back of a feed ledger. Home
  field 3, long field 3, stony half-acre 3, orchard 3.5, cellar 4.
- **The ford lays the flame flat past 9 knots** — end of the string.
- Gran's advice: ask Camber three times, never four. "There's a point where a man like
  that stops helping you and starts defending himself."
- **Registrar Rab Camber**, Third Circuit, 22 years, Field rank, thin and permanently
  damp, folding desk, strongbox, oxhide manual with a missing brass corner.
- Circuit arrived Green 9, two days late ("punctual, by Third Circuit standards").
- 11 children that spring. List alphabetical by farm; Alders farm Whitts, so Wick is 9th.
- Results: Sil Thatcher's girl 9 ft daub. Rennick twins 9 and 9 daub. **Ock** (well-fed
  father, money) 10.5 ft **brick** — square made an appreciative noise.
- Kindling formula is a legal one, ~8 sentences: consent, of age, not indentured.
- A 9x9 room = **1/480th of an acre**.
- **FORM 9-C — "Kindling at a place of the subject's nomination."** Requires: assent of
  attending registrar, witness of age, written nomination by the subject, and **2 copper**
  to defray transport of the frame. Never once requested in Camber's career.
- Gran paid the two copper. Gran witnessed and made her mark. Nomination filed; such
  papers keep for 200 years in a strongbox at **Sennet-on-Weft**.
- Kindled at the ford at dusk, frame's front feet in the water. Half the village present.
- **At the moment of kindling the Wickwater went still** — upstream and down. A travelling
  leaf stopped travelling. **Nobody in the crowd noticed.** (Confirmed later by Wick asking.)

## B01 c003 — "Forty-One Acres"  [Green 9, dusk → Green 10 dawn]
- Kindling does not hurt. Breath goes past where breath goes; a wall is *noticed*, not broken.
- **Wick's Hold:** flat pale hard ground, ridged, crunches underfoot — **salt**. Extends in
  every direction. Sky is **black with nothing in it** — not night-black; ceiling-black.
  **Total silence.** Voice carries about six feet. **No walls. No fire.**
- Salt closes behind his boots; leaves no footprints.
- He walked 1,000 paces inside and passed not one feature. "Nothing had ever happened here."
- The whole kindling took ~4 seconds of real time.
- **The brass frame:** inner hoop = acreage, swung to the limit of its arm and quivered.
  Outer hoop = weight, went round to the pin. Camber reset and re-read **11 times**.
- **ASSESSMENT (first Ledger block in the series):** Acreage **41.2** · Weight **0.0** ·
  Coherence **ragged** · Terrain none · **Fire: none present** · Rank **Ember (unrankable)**.
- Camber crossed out FEET on the form, wrote ACRES, initialled it, then wrote a note
  explaining the note. First time in 22 years he has crossed anything out.
- Camber's line: "He has roughly **twenty thousand rooms**. With nothing in any of them."
- Camber, asked what Wick's fire is: **"I don't know."**
- The village is *kind* about it, which Gran predicted and which is worse than cruelty.
- Gran: "And what do people do with a room? — They put things in it."
- **Turn:** Wick walks his Hold all night trying to find its edge. Does not find it.

## B01 c004 — "The Fire"  [Green 10–17]
- **THE FIRE (system fact, established by Camber):** the Wild present at the moment of
  kindling goes in all at once, collects at the middle, condenses, lights, burns for life.
  The taper does nothing; it is for the form. **The fire IS the weight.** Drawing feeds it.
  A 9-ft room saturates in a season and is never thought about again.
- Wick has no fire → drawing produces nothing. "A cup of water in a lake."
- **Failed of Kindling** (stated by Camber): if the Rust assessment returns the same
  result, the second note is entered. Bars: hunting licence, Hall membership, apprenticeship
  in any licensed craft. Appeal exists. Has succeeded **once, Y-604**, and the man was
  already dead. Clock: **six months, Green → Rust (month 9)**.
- What Embers actually do in Hollow Alder — the village runs on this:
  Sil Thatcher's girl dries daub walls (byre corner, stays dry till autumn; foot rot cost
  the Thatchers 11 shillings the year before last). **Ma Dunnock** predicts hard lambing
  3 days out, right 4 times in 5. **Old Rennick** keeps the well from going brackish.
  Two men can take the ache out of a spent back. Tam finds water.
  Nobody in the village has ever seen a Wildborn or registered a working.
  Two older farmers are Hearth; both got there by being kicked by things.
- Wick tried to dry a wall, the well, a sick ewe, warm his own hands. Nothing.
  "Like pulling on a rope that was not tied to anything."
- **Drawing method** (Gran's, learned from an unkindled woman): breathe out longer than
  you breathe in, think about the bottom of a well. It works — Wick feels the thread. It
  disperses across 41 acres and is unfindable.
- **HOLD FACT 2: there is a middle, and out.** You cannot walk past the middle of your own
  Hold; any return line arrives exactly at the middle regardless of angle. Wick's running
  list of true things about his Hold now has two entries.

## B01 c005 — "Rent"  [Green 14–16]
- **The tenancy:** 40 acres at Whitts, held from the **Chalk estate**, nine-year term with
  four years left, rent **£11 6s payable at Gather** (month 7). Agent: **Nead**.
- Arithmetic: barley 5½ (Gran) or 6 (Tam); wool 2; four spare ewes £1 12s. ≈ £7 10s
  against £11 6s. **Short £3 16s = 912 pennies.**
- Took arrears last year when two households were short. Five are short this year.
  "When there's five, a man like Nead stops being obliging and starts being an example."
- If refused: out at **the Slack**, all four to Marrowfield for wages.
- **Hob Tunny**, carter, Marrowfield ↔ north Loam villages weekly in good weather.
- **BRI'S PARCEL:** brown paper, string, wax, good clear hand — *Alder, Whitts, Hollow
  Alder*. Inside: oilcloth, **eight shillings** in silver. **No note.** Third time money
  has come with no word. Gran is not glad. "A girl who's all right sends a word,
  because a word is free."
- **THE HUNT ECONOMY (via Hob):** Hall has a board; Ledger writes **Writs of Hunt**, Hall
  runs them, licensed bodies take them down and are paid.
  · tallow-crawler: **4 copper** Seed + 3d head
  · chalk-hound: **2 shillings** Seed, bounty on top when bad in the barrows, known to hit 5s
  · nine-in-a-coat (Wold grade): **£8** for the Seed alone
- **Licence:** Ledger issues it on production of a tally at **Hearth or better** with no
  black mark. Costs **2 shillings**.
- **Unlicensed hunting:** fine **4 silver** first offence, rising, plus **a mark on the
  tally**, which follows you.
- **DORN VANCE** named: holds the licence for the whole Loam circuit out of Marrowfield.
  High Wold. Eyes grey all through "like a boiled egg," from the chalk in him. "Not a bad
  man — just no give in him anywhere." Takes your Seeds and walks you to the office himself.
- **THE CATCH-22 AND ITS HOLE:** no writ without a licence, no licence without Hearth, no
  Hearth without weight, no weight without a Seed, no Seed without hunting.
  → The licence governs **taking writs and selling**. **Nobody licenses planting.**
  Wick's realisation. Hunting remains illegal; the loophole is only about the money.
- Wick's total possessions: a boar spear (made by Tam), Gran's tinder-box, his mother's
  9-knot string, 41 acres of salt.
- Nearest known Welling: **Tallow**, two hours the far side of Marrowfield, licensed,
  fenced, farmed, watched. Thin ground does not grow Wildborn.

## B01 c006 — "The Ford"  [Green 18–24]
- Wick makes a **new line: 27 knots** of byre twine and beeswax, gaps matched by a cut
  stick. Gran: "I've been in this house seventy-one years and I've never seen anyone need
  more than six."
- **The ford reads 12 knots.** Checked from four corners: 12, 12, 11½, 12.
- **The profile along the bank** (10-pace steps from the bridge post): 5, 6, 7, 7, 9, **12**
  at the alders, then **5** ten paces downstream.
  **It has an EDGE.** Wild does not do edges; it slopes. This is the tell.
- The **third alder** holds standing water **a hand below the level of the river**, four feet
  away, on soaked gravel. It is draining somewhere.
- Under the third alder: rot has taken the base and the gravel under it; a hole the width
  of Wick's shoulders, angling down into the bank, wet, visible ~8 ft.
- **Cold air comes up steadily.** Smells of nothing — "washed," aggressively absent.
- Candle at the mouth: flame lies flat, points straight up out of the hole, no tremor.
  Lowered on the twine: flat at knot 1 and 2; **out between 3 and 4** — and it did not blow
  out. Wick, wax and all, came up **cold, with no smoke on it.**
- **IT IS A WELLING.** Unlicensed, unrecorded, ~400 yards from the last house in Hollow
  Alder, on ground that runs 3 knots for forty acres in every direction.
- Gran saw him take 40 ft of the good rope and the boar spear and said only "Mm."
- **TURN 1 — Wick's promise, made aloud, alone, in the tree:**
  *"I'm going down there because I want to. Not because it's right."* and
  *"And I'll go to Marrowfield after. Before Gather. That's a promise, and it's a real one,
  and if I break it I'll know I broke it."*
  → He said nothing at dinner, and found it easy, and that frightened him more than the hole.
